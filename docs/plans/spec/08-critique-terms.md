# Critique — verdict: **not-ready** (32 findings)


### [CRITICAL] Part C — C1.4, C2.1, C15.1 read together with C16.1–C16.3 (German AÜG / Dutch WAADI)

**Problem:** The document describes Arbeitnehmerüberlassung and then asserts it is not. C15.1 makes TalentSync the Engineer's employer; C2.1 hands the Client full fachliche Weisungsbefugnis (tasks, priorities, sprint content, architecture, standards, tooling, code review, ceremonies); C1.4 expressly disclaims any deliverable/Werkvertrag obligation. Integration plus client direction plus no work-result obligation is the textbook § 1(1) AÜG fact pattern, and the contract has removed the only structure (Werkvertrag) that would avoid it. C16 then makes it worse: C16.2 places the duty to spot the licensing requirement on the CLIENT, and C16.6 does the same for the UK opt-out. A hirer cannot cure a lender's missing licence: § 9(1)(1) AÜG voids the supply contract and § 10(1) deems an employment relationship between the Engineer and the Client, with joint social-security liability under § 28e(2) SGB IV (assessable up to 4 years, 30 years if intentional) and § 266a StGB exposure for the Client's managing director. TalentSync's only indemnity for this (C15.4) is capped at EUR 50,000 by C19.1, and C15.3 runs the wrong way — the Client indemnifies TalentSync. No German buyer's counsel signs this; and if it is signed and AÜG bites, TalentSync's own recovery position is worthless.

**Fix:** Delete C16.2, C16.6 and C15.4. Replace C16.1–C16.3 with: "C16.1 Supplier's licensing obligation. TalentSync is solely responsible for obtaining and maintaining every licence, permit, authorisation, registration or admission required in any jurisdiction in respect of its supply of Engineers under Part C, including an Erlaubnis zur Arbeitnehmerüberlassung under the German AÜG, registration under Article 7a WAADI and admission under the Dutch Wtta from 1 January 2027. TalentSync warrants that it holds every such authorisation applicable to each Assignment at the date the Assignment Schedule is signed and will maintain it throughout. The Client has no duty to investigate, advise on or notify TalentSync of any such requirement, and no failure by the Client to do so reduces TalentSync's obligation. C16.2 Where an Assignment is or may constitute Arbeitnehmerüberlassung within § 1(1) AÜG, the Assignment Schedule shall, before the Engineer commences Services, (a) expressly designate the supply as Arbeitnehmerüberlassung and (b) name the Engineer, as required by § 1(1) sentences 5 and 6 AÜG. C16.3 Licensing and status indemnity (uncapped). TalentSync shall indemnify the Client in full, and the cap in D6.3 does not apply, against all liabilities, back pay, holiday pay, social-security contributions and interest, tax, fines, penalties, legal and remediation costs arising from (a) breach of C16.1 or C16.2; (b) any determination under § 9(1) or § 10(1) AÜG, Article 7a WAADI, the Wtta or any equivalent provision that an Engineer is or is deemed to be an employee or worker of the Client; or (c) the Client's joint and several liability under § 28e(2) SGB IV, § 14 AEntG, § 13 MiLoG or the Dutch WAS in respect of an Engineer. C16.4 The Client may terminate any Assignment immediately, without charge beyond Charges for Services actually performed, on becoming aware that C16.1 is not satisfied." Keep C15.3 but narrow it to losses caused by the Client's documented breach of C2.2 or C3.2 and make it expressly subject to C16.3 where both apply.


### [CRITICAL] C3.1, C15.1, C16.1 vs src/data/content.ts:207 (FAQ: "Moldova, Romania, Ukraine, and Poland")

**Problem:** The entire licensing analysis in C16.1 rests on one premise — Services performed exclusively remotely from the Republic of Moldova by Engineers on Moldovan tax and social security. The company's own live marketing says it sources from Romania, Ukraine and Poland. A Polish or Romanian Engineer supplied to a German client is intra-EU cross-border labour supply: the AÜG applies to lenders established in other Member States, the Posting of Workers Directive and A1 certification rules engage, and Article 7a WAADI applies to EU-established suppliers with no Dutch establishment. C15.1's warranty (Moldovan tax and social security for every Engineer) is then simply false. Opposing counsel will pull the FAQ page in disclosure and use it to show the parties' own understanding contradicted the recital in C16.1 that they had "concluded that no temporary-agency-work licence is required."

**Fix:** Pick one and make the site and the contract say the same thing. Either (a) restrict Part C in fact to Moldova-resident Engineers and change src/data/content.ts:207 to "We specialise in Moldovan engineering talent, with our headquarters in Chișinău" — and route Romanian/Polish/Ukrainian engineers to Part B (introduction only) where the Client employs them directly; or (b) add an Annex to Part C: "Country Annex. This Annex lists each country from which Engineers may be supplied. For each country TalentSync warrants: the contracting entity, the form of contract with the Engineer, the social-security regime and A1 position, and each authorisation held under C16.1. No Engineer may be supplied from a country not listed in this Annex. TalentSync shall update the Annex on 30 days' notice and the Client may object to an addition without charge." Option (a) is the lazy correct answer until Moldovan counsel has cleared the EU-resident supply chain.


### [CRITICAL] C12.3 (condition precedent) with C12.1, C10.6, C19.1

**Problem:** The IP chain breaks in three places at once, and the client ends up with code it does not own. (1) C12.3 makes the assignment conditional on payment of ALL sums due "including sums due in respect of other Assignments" — a cross-collateralised condition precedent means a single disputed invoice on an unrelated assignment retroactively means title never passed, and on TalentSync's insolvency with any invoice outstanding the Client holds a codebase under a revocable internal-use-only licence. (2) C12.1 is only a warranty that the Engineer contract contains an assignment; if it is false, the Client's remedy is a damages claim capped at EUR 50,000 under C19.1 against a product it cannot lawfully ship. (3) C10.6 contradicts C12.3 outright — it vests "all Work Product for which payment has been made" (per item), while C12.3 vests nothing until everything is paid. Any competent buyer-side counsel treats this as a no-title deal.

**Fix:** Replace C12.3 with: "C12.3 Title passes on payment for the item. Each item of Work Product vests in the Client on payment of the invoice covering the period in which it was created. Non-payment of any sum relating to any other Assignment does not affect title under this Assignment. Where an invoice is disputed in good faith and in writing, title passes on payment of the undisputed portion. Title once vested does not revest on any subsequent breach or termination; TalentSync's remedy for non-payment is the debt, interest, suspension under C8.4 and termination, and not the withholding of title." Delete the second sentence of C10.6 as redundant. Add: "C12.8 Perfection of the chain. On the Client's written request TalentSync shall (a) provide a copy of the intellectual-property provisions of its contract with the relevant Engineer, redacted only as to commercial terms, and (b) procure that the Engineer executes any confirmatory assignment or consent the Client reasonably requires, directly in the Client's favour. TalentSync irrevocably appoints the Client as its attorney to execute such documents in TalentSync's name if TalentSync has not done so within 15 business days of request. TalentSync shall indemnify the Client in full, and the cap in D6.3 does not apply, against all loss arising from any failure of title in the Work Product or from any Engineer asserting any right in it." Also add to C12.2: "Work Product includes inventions, patentable subject matter, database rights, data, model weights, prompts and training-derived artefacts, and know-how reduced to writing." Have Moldovan counsel confirm the form requirement for assignment of author's rights under Law No. 230/2022 and whether email confirmation under D14.1/D21.2 satisfies it — if written signature is required, D14.1 cannot be allowed to vary C12.


### [CRITICAL] D6.3, B19.1, C19.1, C17.1–C17.2, D6.1, D6.5

**Problem:** The liability architecture is unsignable and internally broken. (a) A EUR 50,000 cap (C19.1) sits over the IP infringement indemnity, the confidentiality indemnity and every data-protection failure, while the Client's GDPR exposure is 4% of global turnover and its exposure on infringing code embedded in its product is open-ended. (b) C17.1 advertises EUR 1,000,000 professional indemnity and EUR 1,000,000 cyber cover and C17.2 then walls it off — the Client pays for cover it can never reach, which is also the strongest argument available to a court asked to strike the cap. (c) D6.3 stacks two caps ("capped as set out in B19 and C19, AND … shall not exceed AGGREGATE_ANNUAL_CAP") without saying how they interact; the drafting is ambiguous and ambiguity in a limitation clause is construed against the party relying on it. (d) There is no Kardinalpflicht / essential-obligation carve-out, which is the first thing a German legal team inserts and the absence of which invites the argument that the whole cap fails. (e) D6.1's carve-outs are asymmetric: the Client has uncapped liability for D3.4, B14, C2.2, C3.2 and every indemnity, while TalentSync has none. (f) D6.5's 12-month time bar excludes TalentSync's payment claims but binds the Client's latent code-defect and title claims.

**Fix:** Delete B19.1, C19.1 and C17.2. Replace D6.3 with: "D6.3 Subject to D6.1 and D6.3A, TalentSync's total aggregate liability for all claims arising in any period of 12 consecutive months is the greater of (a) 150% of the sums paid and payable by the Client to TalentSync in that period and (b) EUR 250,000. This is a single cap and no other cap applies. D6.3A Super-cap and uncapped heads. The following are not subject to D6.3: (i) TalentSync's indemnities under C16.3 (licensing and worker status) and C12.8 (title) — uncapped; (ii) TalentSync's indemnity under D7.1 (IP infringement), liability for breach of clause D3, and liability for breach of Applicable Data Protection Law including any administrative fine imposed on the Client to the extent attributable to TalentSync's breach — capped in aggregate at the greater of EUR 2,000,000 and the limits of the insurance required by C17.1. D6.3B TalentSync shall maintain the insurance in C17.1 throughout the term and for 3 years afterwards, shall name the Client's interest on request, and shall not settle any claim in a manner that reduces the cover available to the Client." Add to D6.1: "(g) breach of an obligation the fulfilment of which is essential to the proper performance of the Agreement and on the observance of which the other party may reasonably rely (a cardinal obligation), in which case liability is limited to the loss typically foreseeable at the date of the Agreement." Amend D6.5 to 24 months and add: "This clause does not apply to a claim for payment of sums due, to any liability within D6.1 or D6.3A, or to any claim relating to title in the Work Product." Delete D6.4 (a self-serving recital that a court reads as evidence the terms were not negotiated).


### [CRITICAL] D23.2–D23.5 (exclusive Chișinău jurisdiction, Moldovan law) and the Plain English box in Part D

**Problem:** Three problems, any one of which is fatal. (1) Commercially unsellable: no CTO's legal team at a German or Dutch product company accepts exclusive Moldovan jurisdiction and Moldovan substantive law from a supplier whose product carries AÜG/worker-status risk. This clause alone will cost more deals than every other clause combined. (2) The entire justification (D23.4) rests on a single unverified factual assertion — that Moldova has been a Contracting State to the Hague Choice of Court Convention 2005 since 1 July 2024. If that is wrong, or if it is right but the dispute falls within a Hague exclusion, there is no reciprocal enforcement route and TalentSync has an exclusive forum whose judgments it cannot enforce. (3) Self-harm: TalentSync's most common claim will be an unpaid fee against an EU-domiciled company. By choosing Chișinău exclusively, TalentSync has contracted away the European Order for Payment (Regulation (EC) No 1896/2006) and the European Enforcement Order — the cheapest and fastest routes against precisely those debtors — in exchange for a home forum it will rarely need. D23.3 also contradicts D23.1 (undisputed-debt carve-out) and D23.5 (interim relief anywhere) while stating "neither party may commence proceedings in any other court."

**Fix:** Replace D23.2–D23.5 with: "D23.2 Governing law. The Agreement and any non-contractual obligation arising out of or in connection with it is governed by the substantive law of Austria, excluding its conflict-of-laws rules and the United Nations Convention on Contracts for the International Sale of Goods. D23.3 Arbitration. Any dispute arising out of or in connection with the Agreement, including any question as to its existence, validity or termination, shall be finally settled under the Rules of Arbitration and Mediation of the Vienna International Arbitral Centre (VIAC) by one arbitrator appointed under those Rules. The seat is Vienna, Austria; the language is English. The Expedited Procedure applies where the amount in dispute does not exceed EUR 250,000. The award is final and binding and is enforceable under the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards 1958, to which the Republic of Moldova and every EU Member State are parties. D23.4 Debt carve-out. Nothing in D23.3 prevents either party from pursuing, in the courts of the debtor's domicile, a claim for a liquidated sum that the other has not disputed in writing within 15 business days of the invoice date, including by the European Order for Payment procedure under Regulation (EC) No 1896/2006 where available. D23.5 Nothing in D23.3 prevents either party from applying to any court of competent jurisdiction for interim, protective or injunctive relief, including to restrain a breach of clause D3." Delete D23.6 (a recital with no operative content — the substance now lives in C16.3). If Moldovan governing law is genuinely non-negotiable, keep D23.2 as Moldovan law but the arbitration clause and the debt carve-out are not negotiable: they are what make the terms signable and what actually gets TalentSync paid.


### [CRITICAL] C14.2 ("the parties shall enter into our Data Processing Agreement"), C18.3, D4.3, B18.4, B18.5; Document control ("Data Processing Agreement (on request)")

**Problem:** Part C's data-protection machinery is an agreement to agree and it breaches Article 28 on day one. The Client's DPO will read this as instructing its own organisation to breach Article 28. C14.6 TalentSync shall give the Client 30 days' written notice before engaging any new sub-processor. The Client may object on reasonable data-protection grounds within that period, and the parties shall agree an alternative, failing which the Client may terminate the affected Assignment without charge. C14.7 Notwithstanding C18.3, the Client and its appointed auditor may exercise the audit and inspection rights required by Article 28(3)(h) on 10 business days' written notice, extending to TalentSync's technical and organisational measures, its records of processing and its sub-processor arrangements; the Client shall accept a current ISO/IEC 27001 or SOC 2 Type II report in satisfaction where one is available." Amend C18.3 to read "...does not extend to TalentSync's pricing build-up, margins, or information relating to other clients, and is without prejudice to C14.7." Replace B18.4 with a clause that (i) states that TalentSync's processing of EU candidate and client data falls within Article 3(2) and that TalentSync complies with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 directly, (ii) incorporates SCC Module One with completed Annexes by reference for any client-to-TalentSync transfer, without a separate signature, and (iii) attaches the transfer impact assessment rather than promising it "on request." Fill {{EU_REPRESENTATIVE_DETAILS}} before publication — an unfilled Article 27 token is itself the breach.

### [HIGH] B2 ("Introduction"), B4.2, B4.3, B5.1(b), B5.2, B6.2, B6.5

**Problem:** The fee triggers are drafted so aggressively that they invite an attack on the whole contract rather than on the fee. Delete B5.2. Replace B6.2 with: "B6.2 Where the Client discloses Candidate information to a third party in breach of D3.4 and that third party Engages the Candidate within the Validity Period, the Client shall pay 50% of the Fee as liquidated damages for that breach, and no guarantee under B12 applies to that Engagement." Replace B6.5's presumption with: "Where the Client fails to respond within 10 business days, the Validity Period is suspended until it does, and we may require the Client to procure written confirmation from its payroll provider at the Client's cost."


### [HIGH] B12.1–B12.7 (guarantee), particularly B12.6's nine voiding conditions and B12.5

**Problem:** The guarantee is void in almost every realistic scenario, and a buyer prices it at zero. B12.6(a) voids it if any invoice was ever unpaid on its due date — any invoice, any amount, including one disputed in good faith. (e) voids it for any breach of Part B or Part D, including a trivial slip on B9's 3-day notification. B12.5 Where the Client elects a replacement search, we will commence within 10 business days and conduct it for 60 days; if we have not placed a replacement in that period the Client may then elect a rebate under B12.4, calculated by reference to the original termination date. B12.6 Voiding conditions, exhaustively: (a) an undisputed invoice from us remained unpaid more than 30 days after its due date at the date the Client makes its claim; (b) the Client did not notify us of the termination in writing within 15 days of it; (c) the Engagement terminated by reason of redundancy, restructuring, hiring freeze, insolvency or closure of the Client's business. ### [HIGH] B1.2 (deemed acceptance) and B1.4 ("individually negotiated terms")

**Problem:** B1.2's deemed-acceptance ladder will not incorporate these terms against a European client and will be attacked as the weak point in any fee claim. Limb (b) treats terms merely "made available" — i.e. published on a website — as delivered; limbs (a), (c) and (e) require no delivery of the terms at all, so a client can be "deemed" bound by a document it has never seen. Incorporation of standard terms requires at minimum a clear reference and a reasonable opportunity to read; a website publication plus an unrequested CV does not achieve it, and the drafter's own implementation note concedes the site "binds nobody who never clicked." B1.4 then attempts to escape standard-terms control by declaring four terms "individually negotiated" — a term is only genuinely negotiated where the supplier put its core content up for change, and a countersignature on a pre-printed one-pager does not do that. The device will fail and the assertion is evidence that TalentSync knew the terms were controlled.

**Fix:** Replace B1.2 with: "B1.2 These terms apply where the Client has been sent a copy of, or a durable link to, Parts B, C and D and thereafter (a) signs or confirms in writing a Fee Confirmation, (b) instructs us in writing to source or introduce a Candidate, or (c) an Engagement commences. We shall attach Parts B, C and D to every quotation, every Fee Confirmation and every follow-up to a scheduled meeting, and shall retain evidence of that attachment. Where the Client has not been sent these terms before the first Introduction, they apply only from the date they are sent, and only in respect of Introductions made after that date." Delete limbs (b) and (c) of the existing B1.2 and delete B1.4's "individually negotiated terms" sentence; replace with: "Where a Fee Confirmation is signed, the four terms it states prevail over any inconsistent provision in Part B." Then make the operational change the whole thing depends on: attach the PDF to every quote and Calendly follow-up, and never let an Introduction go out first.


### [HIGH] B17.1 with D5.1 — no undertaking not to re-recruit a placed Candidate or Engineer

**Problem:** The number-one fear of every buyer of recruitment services is the agency coming back for the engineer it placed. The draft not only fails to address it, it expressly reserves the right: B17.1 says "We may introduce any Candidate to any other client at any time, and doing so is not a breach of confidence or of any duty owed to the Client," and D5.1's mutual non-solicit expressly carves Engineers and Candidates OUT. Read together, TalentSync has contracted for the right to poach its own placement out of the client's team the day after the guarantee expires. Buyer-side counsel will read it exactly that way, and it is free to fix — a restriction on TalentSync's own conduct is not a restraint on the individual and raises none of the problems B5.3 and C11.4 are worried about.

**Fix:** Add: "B17.2 We shall not, for 12 months after the Engagement Date, approach, solicit or introduce to any other client any Candidate we introduced to the Client and whom the Client Engaged, or any Engineer supplied to the Client, in each case while that person remains Engaged by the Client or within 6 months of that Engagement ending. C11.8 The undertaking in B17.2 applies equally to Engineers supplied under Part C. B17.3 B17.2 restricts us only. It imposes no restriction on any Candidate or Engineer, who remains free at all times to approach us, to respond to a public advertisement, and to work for whomever they choose; nothing in B17.2 permits us to decline to act for a person who approaches us of their own initiative." Amend B17.1's second sentence to read: "Subject to B17.2, we may introduce any Candidate to any other client at any time."


### [HIGH] B10.2, B10.4, B10.5 (Directive 2011/7/EU and Law No. 66/2025 relied on under a Moldovan choice of law)

**Problem:** The payment clauses invoke statutory regimes the contract has just contracted out of. With Moldovan law chosen under D23.2, the German client's transposition of Directive 2011/7 (§ 288 BGB) does not apply of its own force, so B10.4's "ECB reference rate plus 8 percentage points" is a contractual default rate, not a statutory one — and a contractual default rate in standard terms is attackable as a penalty. B10.2 also mis-states the Directive: Article 3(5)'s 60 days is not an absolute B2B ceiling, it is a default that may be exceeded where expressly agreed and not grossly unfair. B10.5 is worse: "The parties agree that any provision purporting to exclude interest or this compensation would be grossly unfair and is of no effect" is a self-judging recital — grossly unfair is a finding for a tribunal, not a stipulation — and the bracketed drafting note "must never be less than the statutory EUR 40 minimum" has leaked into what will be published as operative text. "Law No. 66/2025 of the Republic of Moldova" is cited without verification, as are Law No. 195/2024 and Law No. 230/2022 elsewhere; a wrong statutory citation in a published legal notice is itself damaging.

**Fix:** Replace B10.4 with: "B10.4 Late payment. Overdue sums bear interest, accruing daily from the due date until payment in full, whether before or after judgment or award, at the higher of (a) the European Central Bank main refinancing rate in force on the first day of the relevant half-year plus 8 percentage points and (b) the rate applicable under any late-payment legislation of the country in which the Client is established, where that legislation applies. Interest accrues without demand." Replace B10.5 with: "B10.5 In addition to interest we are entitled to fixed compensation of EUR 100 for each overdue invoice, and to recover our reasonable further costs of recovery including legal, collection-agency and enforcement costs, to the extent they exceed that fixed sum." Replace B10.2's second sentence with: "Any longer payment period is effective only if expressly agreed in writing and only to the extent it is not grossly unfair to us within the meaning of Article 7 of Directive 2011/7/EU or the equivalent provision of the law applicable to the Client." Before publication, have Moldovan counsel confirm every Moldovan statutory citation in the document (Law 66/2025, Law 195/2024, Law 230/2022, the Civil Code standard-terms and limitation articles) and confirm whether Moldova is in fact a Contracting State to the Hague Choice of Court Convention 2005 — the whole of D23 was built on that one assertion.


### [HIGH] C12.6 (open source) with D7.1 and D7.4

**Problem:** Two commercially serious defects. (1) A blanket ban on the LGPL family is unworkable — weak-copyleft and system libraries under LGPL are present in effectively every runtime and every container base image, so the clause is breached before the first commit and is therefore ignored, which then infects the credibility of C12.6 as a whole. (2) The final sentence excludes all open-source components from the indemnity in D7.1, which given a typical dependency tree means the IP indemnity has a hole roughly the size of the codebase. A buyer reads C12.6 as "you have indemnified me for the 3% of the code you typed." There is also no obligation to disclose what was used until the Client asks.

**Fix:** Replace C12.6 with: "C12.6 Open source. No Engineer shall incorporate into Work Product any component under a licence that would, on the Client's intended distribution or provision of the Work Product, require the Client to disclose, license or make available the source of the Client's own proprietary code — including the GPL, AGPL and SSPL families and any component under those licences that is statically linked or otherwise combined so as to create a derivative work — without the Client's prior written consent. Permissive licences (including MIT, BSD, Apache 2.0 and ISC) and weak-copyleft licences used in unmodified, dynamically linked library form are permitted. We shall deliver with each release, and in any event monthly, a software bill of materials listing every third-party and open-source component in the Work Product with its version and licence. The indemnity in D7.1 applies to any claim arising from our failure to comply with this clause or from any component omitted from the bill of materials; it does not apply to a component the Client approved in writing after disclosure of its licence." Add: "C12.9 AI coding tools. We shall notify the Client of any generative AI coding assistant used in producing Work Product and shall configure it to exclude suggestions matching known public code. The indemnity in D7.1 covers any third-party claim arising from output of such a tool."


### [HIGH] Part C — no exit or transition provisions; C10.6 does the opposite

**Problem:** On termination the Client gets nothing. C10.6 requires it to revoke access immediately and requires TalentSync to procure return or destruction of the Client's confidential information — the entire clause runs towards cutting the cord, and there is no handover, no documentation obligation, no knowledge transfer, no continuity of repository access, no run-book. For a product whose whole proposition is "an engineer joins your existing team," the moment of greatest risk to the Client is the day that engineer leaves, and the contract addresses it by deleting things. Every buyer's procurement team inserts a transition clause; its absence signals that TalentSync has not sold to an enterprise before. It is also a revenue opportunity TalentSync is leaving on the table.

**Fix:** Add: "C10.7 Transition assistance. On termination or expiry of an Assignment for any reason other than the Client's material breach, TalentSync shall, at the Client's written request made within 10 business days, provide up to 20 business days of transition assistance at the rates in the Assignment Schedule, comprising: written handover documentation covering the Engineer's active work, open branches, environments, credentials held, known defects and outstanding decisions; a live handover session with the Client's nominated personnel; and reasonable answers to follow-up questions for 30 days afterwards. Where the Client terminates during the minimum term, transition assistance is chargeable at the standard rate and is not reduced by the Early Termination Charge. C10.8 Before an Engineer's last day, TalentSync shall procure that all Work Product is committed to the Client's repositories and all Client credentials are surrendered." Amend C10.6 so that the destruction obligation takes effect only after C10.7 has been performed or the Client's request window has closed.


### [HIGH] C4.2 and Part C generally — no service levels, no hours of overlap

**Problem:** Part C sells an engineer joining a European client's team from Chișinău, and the contract never once specifies the hours during which that engineer is available to that team. C4.2's list of Assignment Schedule contents includes "the expected hours per week and any cap" but not the working window, the timezone overlap, response times, or attendance at the client's ceremonies (which C2.1 permits the client to require but nothing obliges the engineer to attend). Operationally, overlap hours are the only thing the buyer actually cares about, and it is the single most common source of dispute in this model. Its absence is also what lets C7.2's premium-rate trap fire (see separate finding).

**Fix:** Add to the C4.2 list: "the Engineer's standard working window expressed in Central European Time and the minimum daily overlap with the Client's core hours; the Engineer's committed availability for the Client's stand-up, planning and review ceremonies; the maximum response time to a message during the working window; and the escalation contact at TalentSync outside that window." Add: "C6.7 Where the Engineer is unavailable during the committed working window on more than 3 occasions in any calendar month other than for notified absence under C7.6, the Client may require a service review and, if the position is not corrected within 10 business days, may terminate the Assignment on 5 business days' notice without an Early Termination Charge."


### [HIGH] C7.2 (150% / 200% premium rates)

**Problem:** The premium triggers are defined by wall-clock time in Chișinău, which is one hour ahead of Central European Time. A German client asking for a release at 21:00 CET is asking for work at 22:00 Moldovan time and has just triggered the 200% rate without knowing it. Nobody spots this at signature; everybody fights about it on the first invoice, and the fight costs more than the premium. The clause also has no pre-approval gate — "at the Client's written request" covers a Slack message from any engineer on the client's team, not an authorised budget holder.

**Fix:** Replace C7.2 with: "C7.2 Premium rates apply only where the named Client representative in the Assignment Schedule has approved the work in writing in advance, identifying the hours and the applicable rate. Subject to that approval: work performed outside the Engineer's standard working window stated in the Assignment Schedule, or on a public holiday listed in its annex, is charged at 150% of the standard rate; work performed on a rest day or more than 4 hours outside that window is charged at 200%. Any on-call arrangement is charged as separately agreed in writing. Premium rates are defined by reference to the Engineer's standard working window, not to any absolute local time. Work performed without prior written approval is charged at the standard rate."


### [HIGH] B3.2 ("validated" warranty)

**Problem:** B3.2 is drafted as a hard warranty of four specific steps performed on every candidate described as validated, including "verification of employment history for the preceding 5 years against documentary evidence or direct confirmation from the stated employers." For Eastern European engineers whose former employers have dissolved, been acquired, or operate a no-reference policy, this is routinely impossible. TalentSync will breach this warranty on a meaningful proportion of placements, and each breach gives the client a fee-refund argument, defeats the disclaimers in B16.4 for that head, and supports a misrepresentation claim that D12.2's non-reliance clause does not reach because the representation is inside the contract. This is TalentSync's own exposure, not the client's, and it is entirely self-inflicted.

**Fix:** Replace B3.2 and B3.3 with: "B3.2 Where we describe a Candidate as validated we mean that, before Introduction, we have: (a) conducted a structured technical assessment by or under the supervision of a senior engineer against the technologies in the Client's brief; (b) verified the Candidate's identity against a government-issued identity document; (c) assessed the Candidate's conversational English as sufficient to work in an English-speaking engineering team; and (d) used reasonable endeavours to verify employment history for the preceding 5 years against documentary evidence or direct confirmation from the stated employers. B3.3 We will provide a written validation summary with each Introduction, stating what was verified, by what method, and identifying expressly any element of B3.2(d) that we were unable to verify and why. Where the summary discloses a limitation, we give no warranty in respect of it. Validation is not a background check, criminal-record check, credit check, regulatory clearance, medical assessment or right-to-work determination." Then actually issue the summary — the warranty is safe only if the disclosure happens on every introduction.


### [MEDIUM] B3.4 and E7.4 — {{USES_AI_SCREENING}}

**Problem:** This token is not a fill-in-the-blank, it is a compliance fork, and the draft treats it as cosmetic. Recruitment and candidate-selection systems are an Annex III high-risk use case under the EU AI Act. If the answer is anything other than a clean "no automated screening," Part B needs a full deployer-obligations clause (human oversight, input-data relevance, log retention, worker information, fundamental-rights impact assessment where applicable) and the Candidate Privacy Notice needs Article 22 information. As drafted, both B3.4 and E7.4 have a placeholder sitting inside an operative sentence, so the published document would read "Our current position is: {{USES_AI_SCREENING}}" — a token in a legal notice.

Mirror the first two sentences in E7.4 in the second person. If the honest answer is that AI ranking is in use, do not publish either clause until an AI Act deployer assessment is complete.


### [MEDIUM] D4.4, D8, D13, D23.6, D6.4, B10.5 second sentence, C13.5

**Problem:** Headings with no operative obligation, plus one reference to a document that does not exist. Then write Schedule 4 and add it to the Document control table — a referenced schedule that does not exist is a warranty of nothing.


### [MEDIUM] D15.1–D15.2 (assignment) and D16 (subcontracting)

**Problem:** Both are one-way in TalentSync's favour and both will be redlined. D15.1 bars the Client from assigning even on an internal group reorganisation or a sale of the business, while D15.2 lets TalentSync novate the whole agreement to "any member of our group" — an unnamed entity in an unnamed jurisdiction — on notice alone. For a client whose source code and personal data are involved, an unrestricted right to substitute the counterparty is refused as standard. D16 lets TalentSync subcontract any obligation without consent, which contradicts C4.1's named-Engineer model and conflicts with Article 28(2)'s sub-processor authorisation requirement.

**Fix:** Replace D15 with: "D15.1 Neither party may assign, novate or transfer the Agreement or any right under it without the other's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld or delayed. D15.2 Either party may assign or novate the Agreement, on 30 days' written notice and without consent, to a person acquiring all or substantially all of its business or assets, provided the transferee is not a competitor of the other party and is of at least equivalent financial standing. D15.3 The Client may terminate any Assignment and any current search, without charge beyond sums accrued, within 30 days of notice of a change of control of TalentSync or of a transfer under D15.2." Replace D16 with: "D16.1 We may subcontract performance only to the personal service company of a named Engineer or, with the Client's prior written consent, to another person. We remain fully liable for any subcontractor's acts and omissions as if they were our own. This clause is subject to C14.6 in respect of sub-processors."


### [MEDIUM] E11.2 with A14.2/A14.3 — freelance engineers are not consumers

**Problem:** The consumer carve-outs protect the wrong population. A14.3 and E11.2 both turn on the person "acting outside their trade, business or profession." A freelance software engineer applying for contract work — the exact person Part C is built to engage and the exact person the Careers section invites to email a CV — is acting inside their profession, so neither carve-out reaches them. The result is that A14.2 imposes exclusive Chișinău jurisdiction on individual engineers, which is bad optics for a business whose entire supply is those engineers, and is a term likely to be treated as unfair under Moldova's standard-terms control even in a B2B setting. E1.1 also applies Part E to "any individual" while E11.2's protection applies only to consumers, so Part E promises protection in its Plain English box ("you can use your own country's courts") that its operative clause withholds from most of its readers.

**Fix:** Replace E11.2 with: "E11.2 Part E contains no jurisdiction clause and no choice of law that operates against you. Whether or not you are acting in the course of a trade, business or profession, you are entitled to the protection of the mandatory law of the country in which you habitually reside, you may bring proceedings against us in the courts of that country, and we will bring proceedings against you only in the courts of that country." Amend A14.3 to open: "If you are an individual — whether or not you are acting in the course of a trade, business or profession — this clause does not deprive you of…" and apply the same wording to the last sentence.


### [MEDIUM] A5.2 ("We publish a client name or logo only where we hold that client's permission")

**Problem:** This is stated as a present fact in a published legal notice, and on the research the consents are an open question ({{LOGO_CONSENTS_HELD}}). Ten client names are on the site, including Orange, which operates an active trademark enforcement function, and most enterprise MSAs contain a no-publicity clause making the naming a contract breach independent of trademark law. If a consent does not exist, A5.2 converts a takedown letter into a demonstrably false statement in TalentSync's own terms — and it is the first thing the other side's letter will quote. A5.3's 5-business-day takedown is good and should stay.

**Fix:** Do not publish A5.2's first sentence until the consents exist in writing. Until then replace it with: "A5.2 We publish a client name or logo where we believe we are entitled to do so, and we remove any name, logo, testimonial or case study on request under A5.3 without requiring reasons. Testimonials are genuine statements provided by the named person and are published unedited as to substance; where a testimonial has been shortened, its substance and meaning are unchanged." In parallel, obtain a one-line written reference consent per client and de-identify any client that has not given one ("a European telecommunications operator", "a global mobile operator") in src/data/content.ts. Once every consent is held, restore the original A5.2 wording.


### [MEDIUM] B13.2 (cancellation fee) and B8.5 (10% administrative charge)

**Problem:** Both are penalty clauses dressed as pre-estimates, and both will be reduced by a tribunal and rejected in negotiation. B13.2 charges EUR 2,500 or 25% of the estimated Fee, whichever is greater, if the Client "fails to interview a shortlisted Candidate within 15 business days" — a five-figure charge for being slow, on a contingent search where the client has paid nothing and TalentSync has given no commitment to deliver. No buyer signs a contingent recruitment agreement with a cancellation fee triggered by scheduling. B8.5's 10% administrative charge is calculated on the shortfall rather than on any cost actually incurred, so the "genuine pre-estimate" label is not credible; civil-law courts have a general power to reduce a manifestly excessive contractual penalty and this is the sort of clause that attracts it. C10.3's Early Termination Charge, by contrast, is defensible because it tracks a real committed cost — leave it alone.

**Fix:** Replace B13.2 with: "B13.2 Where the Client withdraws a role after we have delivered a shortlist, we may charge our documented sourcing and assessment costs actually incurred on that role, up to EUR 1,500, on production of a breakdown. No charge arises from the timing of interviews." Replace B8.5's charge sentence with: "…the Fee is recalculated on the correct figure and the difference is payable within 14 days, together with interest under B10.4 from the original due date and our reasonable documented costs of establishing the correct figure." Delete the "genuine pre-estimate / not a penalty" sentence from B8.5 — the label does no work and its presence signals awareness that the charge is exposed.


### [MEDIUM] B18.7 vs E7.2 (candidate retention period) and E4.1 vs E7.2 (lawful basis)

**Problem:** Two data-protection inconsistencies between the client-facing and candidate-facing documents, which is precisely where a regulator or a client DPO looks first. (1) B18.7 states 24 months "reduced to 6 for rejected German-facing candidates" while E7.2 states a flat 24 months. The candidate's Article 13 notice and the client-facing commitment must state the same period; and the parenthetical is a misreading of practice — the ~6-month figure derives from the AGG claim window as a period for which rejected-applicant data should be RETAINED to defend a claim, not a deletion deadline. (2) E4.1 promises to obtain the candidate's "agreement" before sending a CV while E7.2 states legitimate interest as the lawful basis, and B18.7 refers to asking the candidate to "re-consent." Mixing consent and legitimate interest in the same processing is the classic basis-confusion finding, and consent, once used, is withdrawable in a way that undermines the legitimate-interest analysis.

**Fix:** Make both documents say: "We retain Candidate personal data for 24 months from the date of last meaningful contact, after which it is deleted. Where a Candidate has been rejected by a Client, we retain the record of that rejection and the reasons for 6 months from the rejection, or for such longer period as is necessary to defend a claim under applicable equal-treatment law, and then delete it." Replace B18.7's re-consent wording with "…after which it is deleted, unless the Candidate has asked us to continue to hold it." Amend E4.1 by adding: "Your agreement under this clause is a commitment we make to you about how we work; it is not the legal basis on which we process your data. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in matching engineers to roles, as explained in the Candidate Privacy Notice, and withdrawing your agreement under this clause does not affect that basis."


### [MEDIUM] C7.6 (absence) and C5.1–C5.3 (substitution) — no substitute obligation for ordinary sickness

**Problem:** C7.6 makes sickness absence non-chargeable but creates no obligation to cover it. The substitute right in C7.6's final sentence is triggered only by planned absence exceeding 10 consecutive business days, and D9.3(c)'s substitute obligation is triggered only by a force majeure event. So an Engineer off sick for six weeks leaves the Client with no engineer, no substitute, no termination right (the Assignment is not in breach), and a team that has lost its sprint. It also leaves TalentSync with six weeks of zero revenue on that Assignment and no contractual route to reallocate. Both sides lose.

**Fix:** Add to C7.6: "Where an Engineer is absent for any reason, planned or unplanned, for more than 10 consecutive business days, we shall offer a substitute Engineer of equivalent seniority at the same rate within a further 10 business days, and C5.1's notice period does not apply. If we do not offer a suitable substitute within that period, the Client may terminate the Assignment immediately on written notice without an Early Termination Charge, and the minimum term in C10.1 does not apply."


### [MEDIUM] D11.1–D11.2 (notices)

**Problem:** Email is deemed received at the moment of transmission provided no bounce is received, and where no address is stated in a Fee Confirmation the fallback is "the address from which the Client last corresponded with us on the matter." That could be a junior engineer's inbox, an out-of-office, or a shared alias. For an ordinary operational message that is fine; for a notice that starts the 7-day suspension clock, terminates an Assignment, or crystallises a five-figure debt, it is contestable and TalentSync is the party that will have to prove service. A notice served under this clause is valid only if the sender retains a copy of the sent message. Each party shall state a notice address and a legal-notice address in every Fee Confirmation and Assignment Schedule." Amend D11.1 to require those two addresses as mandatory fields.


### [LOW] A6.3(b) vs A6.4(a)

**Problem:** A6.3(b) expressly permits reproduction of Site extracts in AI-generated "answers, summaries, comparisons and recommendations" with attribution, and A6.4(a) prohibits extraction for "any product or service that competes with TalentSync."
